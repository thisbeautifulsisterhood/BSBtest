        <div class="bsb-map-intro-copy">
          <h1>A Spatial Introduction to the Sisterhood</h1>

          <p>
            This lesson allows students to explore in person or virtually the
            <em>This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books</em> site and map data of the 1884
            World’s Fair located in Audubon Park, New Orleans, LA. The BSB site is a
            public-facing digital humanities site that works to educate the public about
            the World’s Fair and give students a space to research and public work.
            A challenge that the <em>This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books Project</em>
            faces is <em>disembodiment</em>. The digital site does not always do a good
            job of showing in a virtual space what occurred historically in a physical
            space (Audubon Park, New Orleans). In this lesson, students will learn more
            about the physical site of the World’s Fair and locate the Woman’s Department.
          </p>

      <div class="bsb-map-content">

        <h2 class="bsb-map-section-title">Instructions</h2>
        <p class="bsb-map-instructions-intro">To implement this lesson, follow these steps.</p>

        <article class="bsb-map-step">
          <div class="bsb-map-step-icon" aria-hidden="true">◉</div>
          <div>
            <h3>Step #1 – Before Class – At Home</h3>
            <p>
              Review the
              <a href="{{ '/' | relative_url }}">This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books Project</a>
              by clicking on the “search” and “learn” menu tabs and exploring the site.
            </p>
          </div>
        </article>


        <article class="bsb-map-step">
          <div class="bsb-map-step-icon" aria-hidden="true">▦</div>
          <div>
            <h3>Step #2: On Location or Virtual – During Class</h3>

            <h4>Option #1 – During Class – Explore Audubon Park in New Orleans, LA.</h4>

            <p>
              In groups, walk through
              <a href="https://audubonnatureinstitute.org/audubon-park" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                Audubon Park
              </a>
              using this
              <a href="https://neworleanshistorical.org/tours/show/14" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                walking tour
              </a>
              by Miki Pfeffer and the University of New Orleans History Department,
              “Audubon Park: Site of the 1884 Cotton Centennial Exposition Tour.”
            </p>

            <p>
              As you review the walking tour, write down your findings using the primary-source
              <a href="https://www.archives.gov/files/education/lessons/document-analysis/english/analyze-a-map-intermediate.pdf"
                 target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                Analyze a Map
              </a>
              graphic organizer from the
              <a href="https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/worksheets/analyze-a-map-intermediate"
                 target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                National Archives
              </a>.
            </p>

            <h4>Option #2 – During Class – Explore Audubon Park Virtually</h4>

            <p>
              In groups, use the same
              <a href="https://neworleanshistorical.org/tours/show/14" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                walking tour
              </a>
              virtually and record your findings with the National Archives map-analysis organizer.
            </p>
          </div>
        </article>


        <article class="bsb-map-step">
          <div class="bsb-map-step-icon" aria-hidden="true">◇</div>
          <div>
            <h3>Step #3 – At Home</h3>

            <p>
              Read and annotate one of the readings from the
              <a href="{{ '/teach.html' | relative_url }}"><em>This Beautiful Sisterhood</em> Bibliography</a>
              that fits best with your course’s theme. You must include
              <em>8–12 annotations</em> throughout the research piece that indicate
              you have read the entire reading.
            </p>

            <p>
              As you annotate, respond to the reading while considering the prompt:
              “How does the map tour and the <em>This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books Project</em>
              demonstrate the concept of space, place? What challenge do digital recovery projects
              face in terms of creating space, place, and embodiment/disembodiment?”
            </p>

            <p>
              For more information about “space” and “place,” see Denise Lawrence-Zuniga,
              <a href="https://www.oxfordbibliographies.com/view/document/obo-9780199766567/obo-9780199766567-0170.xml"
                 target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">
                “Space and Place”
              </a>
              in <em>Oxford Bibliographies in Anthropology</em>.
            </p>

            <p>
              Instructor’s Note: Annotations may be completed on paper or digitally using tools like
              <a href="https://web.hypothes.is/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Hypothes.is</a>.
            </p>
          </div>
        </article>


        <article class="bsb-map-step">
          <div class="bsb-map-step-icon" aria-hidden="true">▣</div>
          <div>
            <h3>Step #4 – During Class or Virtual</h3>

            <p>
              Contextualize what you have learned about the 1884 World’s Fair and Woman’s Literary
              Department so far using the data from the walking tour, chapter, and the
              <a href="{{ '/' | relative_url }}"><em>This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books Project</em> site</a>.
            </p>

            <p>In groups, as a class, or in an online discussion forum, discuss:</p>

            <ul>
              <li>What is the mission of the BSB site?</li>
              <li>What methods make the BSB site a data project, a digital archive, and a recovery project?</li>
              <li>How does the project use and communicate data?</li>
              <li>
                What kinds of challenges occurred when creating the BSB collection?
                How did they work around these challenges? Why are these solutions imperfect?
              </li>
              <li>
                Explore the photos across the walking tour. What can they tell us about
                the 1884 World’s Fair?
              </li>
              <li>
                Make recommendations for how the <em>This Beautiful Sisterhood</em> Project
                can overcome the disembodiment issue while honoring and not simply recreating
                the walking tour.
              </li>
            </ul>
          </div>
        </article>

      </div>
