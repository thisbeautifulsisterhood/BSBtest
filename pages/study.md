      <div class="bsb-study-discovery-text">

        <h2>
          Discovery &amp; Recovery
        </h2>

        <p>
          <em>This Beautiful Sisterhood of Books</em> is a collaborative
          digital humanities project. Our purpose is to recover the contents
          and history of the Woman’s Literary Department from the 1884
          World’s Fair and “recover the exclusion” of Black women and their
          work from the original exhibit.
        </p>

        <p>
          By exploring these exhibits, short essays, and original documents,
          you will learn about . . .
        </p>

        <ul>
          <li>early women’s rights history,</li>
          <li>early Black organizing,</li>
          <li>women and library history,</li>
          <li>women and bibliographic activism,</li>
          <li>women in world’s fair and exposition culture,</li>
          <li>archival recovery.</li>
        </ul>

        <p>
          We encourage you to read between the lines, and reflect on . . .
        </p>

        <ul>
          <li>
            how (and why) the 1884 Woman’s Department strove to define —
            and delimit — the concepts of “Woman” and “Womanhood”; and
          </li>

          <li>
            how race, class, and regional politics shaped gendered meanings
            in the 1884 Woman’s Department and in our digital reconstruction.
          </li>
        </ul>

      </div>

    </div>

  </section>
